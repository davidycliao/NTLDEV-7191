## Course Schedule

### Week 1: Introduction to Quantitative Text Analysis 

Introduces the basic concepts of quantitative text analysis and natural language processing, outlines course structure and expectations, and surveys the text-as-data paradigm in social science research.

### Week 2: Text Preparation in R and Python: Collaborative Workflow

Covers the use of R and Python for text analysis, sets up development environments (RStudio and Positron), and establishes a GitHub-based coding workflow with written coursework submitted through shared Overleaf projects. Introduces supplied corpora and a basic data-feasibility checklist for students' research projects.

### Week 3: Mid-Autumn Festival (No Class)

No class on September 25.

### Week 4: Tokenisation, Document-Feature Matrices, Dictionaries, and Sentiment Analysis

Connects preprocessing decisions and bag-of-words representations to dictionary-based measurement. A single workflow moves from a documented corpus to tokens, document-feature matrices, sentiment scores, and validation in political texts. Students also check corpus boundaries and access conditions for potential projects.

### Week 5: National Day Observed (No Class)

No class on October 9.

### Week 6: Human Coding and Document Classification

Covers human coding, training-set construction, supervised document classification, validation, and performance metrics. Students connect coding rules and annotation quality to the substantive claims a classifier can support.

### Week 7: Supervised, Unsupervised, and Semi-Supervised Scaling

Introduces Wordscores, Wordfish, and Latent Semantic Scaling (LSS) for estimating positions from text, with attention to anchors, assumptions, and external validation. **Individual research abstract due: October 23.**

### Week 8: Topic Models and Their Variants

Covers LDA, structural topic models, and keyword-assisted topic models, with emphasis on interpreting topics, comparing issue attention, and validating topics as social-science measures.

### Week 9: Describing and Comparing Texts: Readability, Similarity, and Word Embeddings

Combines readability and complexity measures with count-based similarity, word embeddings, and context-specific meaning. Students compare how different representations support research on political language across actors and groups.

### Week 10: NLP Pipelines and Transformers: Annotation, Classification, and Interpretability

Connects POS tagging, named entity recognition, and dependency parsing to contextual representations and transformer-based classification. Fine-tuning and interpretability are introduced through applications and demonstrations, with detailed implementation reserved for supplementary materials.

### Week 11: NTU Sports Day (No Class)

No class on November 20.

### Week 12: Large Language Models and Multi-Agent Systems

Introduces LLMs for annotation, classification, summarization, and multi-agent workflows. Applications foreground reproducibility, validation, bias, and the limits of substituting model outputs or simulations for human evidence.

### Week 13: New Forms of Textual Data: Multilingual and Multimodal Sources

Explores research with multilingual texts, political speech recordings, and audiovisual sources. Students examine how machine translation, speech recognition, and model-generated annotations change the evidence available for political and social research, using published applications and a guided comparison of original and transformed text. **Midterm research design report due: December 4.**

### Week 14: Poster Workshop: Format, Visual Presentation, and 5-Minute Talk

This session focuses on poster format, visual presentation, and the 5-minute talk. We will discuss revision directions collectively as preparation for the formal poster presentations in Week 15.

### Week 15: Final Poster Presentations

Students present final research posters, deliver 5-minute presentations, join in-class discussion, and provide peer feedback.

### Week 16: Constitution Day Holiday (No Class)

No class on December 25. Students revise their projects independently after the poster session. **Manuscript due: TBA. Bonus claim deadline: TBA.** Both deadlines will be announced on NTU COOL. Submit the final research paper through Overleaf with instructor editing access; include any bonus claim and its supporting documents in a shared Overleaf project.
